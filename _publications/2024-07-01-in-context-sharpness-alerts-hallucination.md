We address the problem of hallucination detection in large language models through the lens of **sharpness in inner representations**. Our method leverages the observation that hallucination-prone inputs lead to sharper attention patterns and reasoning activations compared to normal inputs. We observe that hallucination-prone cases in instruction-following tasks produce distinct sharpness signatures in the attention and FFN layers.

We discover a universal truthfulness hyperplane inside large language models (LLMs) that separates truthful responses from untruthful responses in the model's parameter space. Our key findings include:

**Universal Hyperplane**: The representation space of LLMs contains a hyperplane that separates truthful and untruthful responses across different tasks and prompt variations, suggesting an underlying invariant in the model's parametric knowledge.

**Synthetic Task Validation**: We demonstrate the hyperplane's existence using latent synthetic tasks constructed via prompting, showing that the geometric property holds independently of surface form or content.

**Interpretability Insights**: The hyperplane reveals representational structures that govern truthfulness beyond traditional token-level or token-probability-based approaches, providing a new lens on interpretability.

**Pure Activation Boundary**: Unlike many interpretability methods that require adding perturbations or measuring indirect effects, our approach identifies the boundary using pure residual-stream activations, making it a cleaner and more generalizable finding.
